# Defense (D/ST) strategy — standing reference

Written after mock draft #3 exposed that we had no data or recommendations on *when or which*
defense to draft (`roster-philosophy.md` holds the "draft exactly one DEF" standing rule from
the 0-DEF incident; this page covers everything past that rule). League context that shapes all
of this: best-ball (Sleeper auto-starts the DEF slot — no weekly start/sit effort needed), one
hard `DEF` starting slot, no kicker, and this league's team-defense scoring includes
points-allowed tiers, sacks, INTs, fumble recoveries, safeties, blocked kicks, and ST TDs
(`PROJECT_PLAN.md` §3). New kickoff rules incentivize returns, which raises special-teams TD
variance leaguewide.

## When to draft a defense: later than everyone else, on purpose

The single best-supported finding across sources: **being early on defense is a losing play.**

- Historical ADP-vs-outcome data (NBC Sports/Rotoworld 2026, multi-year): D/STs drafted top-4
  by ADP very rarely finish as the season's #1 fantasy defense — drafting one of the top-3
  defenses by ADP gave roughly a **3.8%** chance of landing the actual #1 defense.
- Yet the market drafts them early anyway. 2026 CBS ADP (12-team, half-PPR-ish formats):
  DEN ≈ pick 80 (**round 7**), HOU ≈ 94 (round 8), LAR/SEA ≈ 105 (round 9), MIN ≈ 114
  (round 10), PHI ≈ 122 (round 11), PIT/NE/LAC/DET/BAL ≈ picks 139–146 (rounds 12–13),
  everyone else ≈ pick 153+ (round 13+).

**Our play: let someone else pay round 7–9 prices for DEN/HOU/LAR/SEA.** Target our DEF pick
in **rounds 12–14**, taking the best available tier-2/tier-3 unit whose first 2–3 scheduled
weeks are favorable. This refines (not replaces) the `roster-philosophy.md` standing rule
("draft exactly one DEF, rounds 10–15"): the round window stands; the refinement is *which end*
of the window to prefer and not reaching for a tier-1 name just because it's sliding.

Other timing rules worth keeping:

- **Never be the first manager to draft a defense** — if a D/ST run starts, take skill players
  and grab whoever's left from the tier below a round later.
- **Draft exactly one defense, never two** out of the draft (holding two in-season for bye-week
 /streaming coverage is fine; bench spots are better spent on RB/WR/TE upside in August).
- In-season, evaluate defenses **2–3 weeks at a time by schedule**, not by season-long rest-of-
  season rank — the D/ST landscape churns too fast. Streaming by matchup is viable from week 1;
  a bad real-life defense with a soft schedule (e.g. NO last season) can be plenty usable.

## What actually predicts fantasy defense success

NBC's offseason study of every top-10 fantasy defense over five seasons — stats most common to
those units, in order:

1. First downs allowed
2. Points per game allowed
3. Third-down conversion rate allowed
4. Pressure rate (more predictive than raw sack totals)
5. Passes defended
6. Takeaway rate
7. Explosive-play rate allowed (20+ yard plays)

Practical use mid-season: a unit pressuring at a top-rate but with unlucky turnover luck is a
buy-low; a unit living off turnovers with poor underlying rates is a sell-high.

## Draft-day checklist (fold into `draft board` usage)

1. Track whether a D/ST run has started; if yes, defer.
2. From round 12, check the remaining board against this page's tier table + each candidate's
   weeks 1–3 schedule.
3. Take exactly one; do not spend bench slots on a second.
4. Post-draft: identify weeks 1–3 streaming alternatives off the wire before week 1, so a bad
   opening matchup has a pre-planned answer.
